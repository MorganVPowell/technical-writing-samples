# Reducing-Support-Calls-15-by-Rewriting-Unclear-KB-Articles

Context:
I worked in senior technical support, where a set of knowledge base articles covered a commonly used product area. These articles were the first thing users and internal staff turned to before contacting support.

Problem:
The articles were written in a way that was difficult to follow. Even experienced coworkers could not parse them, which meant customers had even less chance of understanding them. As a result, users skipped self-service entirely and called support instead. Call volume on this topic was rising, and the same questions came up repeatedly.

Action:
I audited the affected articles and identified the specific points of confusion, including unclear terminology, missing steps, and explanations that assumed too much prior knowledge. I documented these issues with concrete examples and relayed them to the technical team. Working with them, I proposed clearer phrasing and a more logical structure for each article. The technical team rewrote and published the updated versions.

Result:
After the rewritten articles went live, support calls on that topic dropped by approximately 15%. Users were able to resolve the issue through self-service, and the support team spent less time answering questions the documentation should have handled.

Takeaway:
Documentation is not just reference material. When it fails the reader, it shifts work onto the support team and creates a worse experience for users. Identifying where docs break down, and working with subject matter experts to fix them, directly reduces support load and improves self-service. This experience is what drew me to technical writing as a career.
